# ReservKit Competitor Marketing Review

Reviewed October 3, 2026. Scope: eight relevant rental/tour platforms, not an exhaustive census. "Rez" interpreted to include both Rezdy and Rezgo.

## Method and Limits

Read official homepages, selected industry pages, demo entry points and switching content. Visually inspected rendered desktop homepages for FareHarbor and Booqable; captured Checkfront and Peek for review. Compared against ReservKit's local marketing update and app FEATURE_INVENTORY. No competitor accounts created, forms submitted, or payments made.

Public marketing shows positioning and available conversion paths, not traffic sources, ad spend, acquisition costs, actual conversion rates or customer outcomes. Vendor performance figures and integration claims are vendor assertions, not independently tested results. No competitor price comparison or savings guarantee is established here.

## ReservKit: Present vs Missing

Already present: explicit watersports positioning, published subscription/booking-fee structure, Free-first signup, docs, switching guidance, pricing FAQ, actual product screens, and a guided-setup request form. The guided-setup page already promises a reply within one business day; confirm operational coverage rather than invent another support promise.

Prepared locally, not deployed: five-industry navigation, all nine SEO footer links, six authentic Clearwake screenshots, industry-appropriate image selections, demo/AI-photo disclosure.

Main marketing gaps:

1. **First-screen clarity.** Current hero is a long feature inventory. At 1440x1000 it consumes seven headline lines and puts the CTA low. Keep the preferred side-by-side layout, shorten the heading and move details to supporting text.
2. **Readable product evidence.** Whole-screen screenshots are authentic but their text is tiny at marketing display sizes. Add a real 60-90 second narrated flow and focused screenshots with full-screen expansion. Do not paint fake UI or fabricate signed waivers.
3. **Independent proof.** Clearwake is demonstration data, not customer traction. Add a factual LAWS operator story only after validating actual use and obtaining approval for figures/photos; disclose common ownership. Later add permissioned independent operator quotes.
4. **Assisted conversion.** Guided setup exists but is less prominent than signup. Promote it next to signup and spell out the next step. Do not advertise instant scheduled demos until scheduling capacity exists.
5. **Human credibility.** A genuine founder/operator photo and specific operating experience can be stronger than another generated lifestyle graphic. Use owner-provided or properly licensed real photos.
6. **Distinct industry pages.** Go beyond swapped nouns: jet ski craft/passenger pricing; boat group bookings and day-of readiness; kayak guest counts and waivers. State limits where needed.
7. **Switching specificity.** Explain supported customer/future-booking CSV import, what requires help, and the test-before-switch sequence. Avoid universal one-click migration promises.

## Product Gaps Are Not Website Tasks

The app inventory marks waitlist as future, not live. It describes equipment as readiness inventory, with resource-type capacity blocking, physical-unit assignment, scanning and a deeper inventory ledger still future. Those distinctions matter when comparing rental platforms.

OTA/channel-manager networks and native mobile apps promoted by competitors are not verified ReservKit equivalents in this review. Do not add partner logos, claim those capabilities, or market platform parity without separate verification. Likewise, a refundable charged damage deposit is not a card authorization hold.

Do not turn these differences into a pre-launch feature spree. Qualify early operators against current workflows; exclude requirements we cannot support. Revisit product gaps when multiple suitable prospects identify the same blocker.

## Recommended Next Batch

1. Keep the design structure; propose the literal heading **Boat & watersports booking software**. Supporting text should explain the guest-booking-to-dock-check-in workflow.
2. Keep **Start free** primary. Make **Request guided setup** an obvious secondary path, using the existing form. Put **Watch the walkthrough** with the product demonstration once the video exists.
3. Lead product evidence with a readable watersports booking catalog and one operator task. Keep complete screenshots available for inspection.
4. Publish a truthful founder/operator section, followed by a verified LAWS case study when evidence is available.
5. Strengthen the three priority landing pages: boats, jet skis, kayaks. Each needs a specific scenario, screenshot, pricing explanation, objections and appropriate CTA.
6. Validate the lead funnel: source -> qualified inquiry -> demo/setup -> activated organization -> first legitimate booking/payment. Existing analytics are not proof that every step is measured.
7. Begin a small founder-led cohort of 5-10 well-matched operators. Offer scoped assisted setup under existing pricing; no new discount or free-service commitment has been authorized. Use real objections to improve the site before broad paid acquisition.

Suggested copy is a recommendation, not implemented by this research task. No homepage rewrite, pricing change, ads, outreach or production release performed.
